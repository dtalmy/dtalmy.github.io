Research

We use mathematical models to study how aquatic microorganisms grow, interact, and die. Our work spans ocean and freshwater systems, focusing on the ecological and biogeochemical consequences of microbial processes at the scale of individual cells through to global ocean cycles.

A central theme is understanding mortality in microbial communities — including loss to viruses, grazers, pathogenic bacteria, and oxidative stress — and how these processes shape elemental cycling, community composition, and ecosystem function. We develop mechanistic models grounded in physiology and test them against laboratory and field data.

Current interests include phytoplankton stoichiometry and photoadaptation, virus–host population dynamics, microzooplankton grazing, and the integration of microbial processes into Earth system models.
